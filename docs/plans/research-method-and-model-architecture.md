# Evidence method, hybrid models, and moderated discussion

The central functional specification is [functional.md](../../functional.md). This document elaborates its evidence and orchestration contracts; implementation choices must preserve that functional behavior.

Status: personal-use research requirements and architecture proposals, updated 8 November 2026. Compass is a neutral theological research tool: the user investigates questions and weighs arguments; the app does not certify religious truth. No models or API integrations have been run for this project. This document extends the main plan and supersedes its original local-only coordinator assumption, deferral of manuscript viewing and image-to-text reading, and heavy release-evaluation program. Provider setup uses practical connection, capability and quality checks.

## One evidence method for all three collections

Apply the same standards to the selected Torah, Bible, and Quran collections. State the exact edition, manuscript witness, translation, and canon/scope. Torah means the selected Torah corpus; do not silently expand it to the entire Tanakh. A Bible collection must state its canon. An ancient manuscript is a textual witness, not automatically an author's original or proof that a statement came directly from a prophet.

For a substantive interpretive answer, produce an inspectable evidence argument:

1. **Question and scope:** what is being asked, which texts and historical period are relevant, and what the proposed answer would actually establish.
2. **Primary text:** retrieve the passage and surrounding literary unit from the named edition, or inspect the selected image and its identified transcription revision. Keep the original image, published transcription, machine reading, user correction, editorial reconstruction, published translation and generated translation distinct.
3. **Context within the collection:** seek related passages and counterevidence across the selected collection, not only verses supporting a preferred conclusion. Show relevant changes of speaker, genre, addressee, and textual setting. Whole-collection context is a research obligation, not a claim that every verse was exhaustively inspected on every turn. Record search scope and gaps.
4. **Evidence items:** list E1, E2, E3, etc. Each item records the observation/claim, source and location, date or period where relevant, method, uncertainty, and whether independent corroboration exists. Distinguish directly observed text, a catalog attribution, a scholarly hypothesis, and an inference. Calling something a fact does not establish it.
5. **Historical and linguistic context:** use traceable evidence for contemporary word usage, material culture, social setting, genre, and transmission. Academic work is a useful reference; authentic material from identifiable authors, projects, communities and collections can also be examined in its stated role. Separate the date of a physical witness, the proposed date of composition, and the period a narrative describes. Do not infer an ink or composition date from a parchment date alone.
6. **Alternative readings:** explain how plausible readings depend on vocabulary, syntax, translation, textual variant, literary context, historical reconstruction, or theological assumptions. Attribute a position narrowly to its named source, school, or author; do not claim an entire religion speaks with one voice.
7. **Argument and limits:** show which evidence supports or weakens each reading, the assumptions connecting it to the conclusion, and what would change that assessment. Offer the best-supported reading for the stated historical/textual question when warranted, without inventing certainty or a consensus. Historical analysis does not experimentally establish supernatural truth claims.

The UI displays a concise evidence argument, not a fabricated transcript of hidden model reasoning. Use **Evidence / Context / Interpretations / Assessment / Open questions** in Research, with an expandable equivalent in Chat. A comparison table includes reading, supporting evidence, counterevidence, assumptions, source, and uncertainty. Do not assign pseudo-precise confidence percentages without a defined measurement method. A well-established observation and an unsupported assertion need not receive equal credibility merely to appear balanced.

## Primary texts and secondary material

Primary scripture is the starting evidence for textual interpretation. Commentaries and later traditions are not automatically authoritative explanations. Academic scholarship and other identifiable sources supply separately attributed linguistic, material, historical and interpretive material. When the user asks why interpretations differ, a documented interpretation can be described as an object of comparison without adopting its religious authority.

For personal research, any authentic, traceable source available online can be considered: text editions, scans, articles, author websites, project repositories, archives, religious or community publications, and reliably sourced copies. An institutional publisher is useful provenance, not an exclusive entry requirement. Check who produced or transmitted the material, its edition or object identity where relevant, and the claim for which it is being used. An authentic document can contain disputed claims, errors or a partisan interpretation. Unknown provenance remains a research lead rather than established evidence. The specific hadith restriction below still applies.

Record source URL, author/owner, edition or date where available, access time and relevant use limits as part of ordinary research. Missing training or public-distribution permission does not block personal reading. Preserve accessible material locally for reference when the actual access/use conditions permit it; keep known restrictions on storage, cloud processing and sharing distinct. Public accessibility alone is not blanket permission for every later use, and the app does not bypass access controls.

For Quran analysis, hadith is excluded from the default corpus, retrieval, and interpretive justification. Tafsir is not a default explanatory authority. The user's only possible hadith exception is individually admitted material with demonstrable direct provenance under an explicit evidence criterion, and then solely as historical context, never as authority interpreting or overriding the Quran. A traditional label such as sahih does not automatically satisfy that admission criterion. No hadith pack is approved by this plan; if the required provenance cannot be established, exclude it. Do not imply that the system can automatically prove exact attribution to Muhammad. A webpage quoting excluded material cannot bypass this policy by laundering it into supposedly primary evidence.

Apply the same distinction between primary text and later interpretive authority to Torah and Bible analysis, including later commentary and tradition. Historical claims about any tradition receive the same evidentiary scrutiny. The app can explain documented interpretive differences without silently changing the configured source policy.

When comparing earlier prophets or revelations, examine relevant passages in each collection and report their own claims, agreements, and differences. A Quran passage about earlier prophets is evidence of the Quran's position, not permission for the app to overwrite a Jewish or Christian text's own context. Do not force the conclusion that every later doctrine or formulation is identical. Do not encode a preferred theological harmonization as religious impartiality.

Design references checked as primary passages on 8 November 2026 include [Quran 2:136](https://quran.com/2/136), [3:3](https://quran.com/3/3), [4:82](https://quran.com/4/82), and [5:48](https://quran.com/5/48). These are research leads about prophets, prior revelation, reflection, and differences. The product's holistic research protocol is an explicit method choice, not a claim that one verse literally specifies this software algorithm. The linked pages' embedded tafsir is not admitted by linking the primary passage, and no translation reuse rights follow from these references.

## Manuscripts in the research workspace

Manuscript viewing is part of the core Research experience. Provide a **Manuscripts** tab with zoom/pan, folio and recto/verso navigation, image type, holding institution or identified owner/project, shelfmark or object ID where available, dating attribution, attribution/use notice, and a direct source link. Show a sourced transcription and a named translation alongside an image when available. Mark absent transcription, missing leaves, uncertain readings, corrections, and editorial reconstructions rather than inventing content. Passage-to-folio/region mapping must be evidenced; do not invent an exact image highlight from a modern verse number.

Use an extensible source register that records the provenance of the actual manuscript or image. Institutional catalogs and scholarly collections are preferred reference points; an identified owner, author, project, delegated host or authentic traceable copy can also supply material. A web search hit or a familiar logo alone does not establish authenticity. Compare object details and image provenance with original catalogs or other independent records where available, and label gaps. See the [starting register and source checks](../sources/manuscript-register.md).

Prefer IIIF-compatible manifests and image services where supported; [IIIF Presentation API](https://iiif.io/api/presentation/3.0/) describes structured presentation of digital objects. Inspect metadata, access behavior and actual use conditions when connecting a source. Use local reference copies or remote viewing according to those conditions. If embedding or copying is restricted, keep the source record and open its viewer. Images must be genuine reproductions, never AI-generated manuscripts passed off as evidence. The user can also inspect a personal photograph whose object provenance is not yet known; the source record keeps that uncertainty visible. Direct image-to-text reading is a core workflow under R28.

## Reading text from photos and scans

The user can import a photograph or scan, or select an image already consulted through a source. Choose a page or region, request a machine reading, inspect it beside the image, correct it and then translate, search or investigate the selected transcription. OCR, handwriting recognition (HTR) or a configured image-capable model can supply that reading where the selected route supports the material. Hebrew/Aramaic, Greek and Quranic Arabic are target research scripts; printed text, handwriting, historical letterforms and mixed-direction layouts need separate capability notes. No route is presumed to read every script or photograph accurately.

Preserve the original bytes and hash. Rotation, contrast changes and crops are derived views with their relationship to the original recorded. A transcription revision records the input image/region, script or language choice, engine/model version and reading status. A suggested script/language can be changed by the user. Where the reader returns actual line or word coordinates, store them with the relevant image dimensions and transformations; otherwise show page/region-level links rather than invented word highlights. Omitted regions remain identifiable as not read.

The first machine reading is unreviewed. Mark uncertain characters, competing readings and unreadable spans; do not silently fill gaps from a familiar scripture passage or turn a translation into a supposed literal transcription. Any requested reconstruction is a separate proposal. A supplied scholarly transcription is an attributed comparison layer, not hidden input that makes missing letters appear to have been recognized from the image. Corrections create new revisions without overwriting the automatic reading. A user-reviewed label identifies only the text or region actually checked. Successful OCR does not certify a canonical quotation, the object's authenticity or date, or the truth of its contents.

Select an available local reader or an eligible configured image-processing route. The user sees whether the selected crop or the full image will be processed, locally or by a named provider, before an external call. A text-only connection is not enough. Existing capability, data-choice, budget, cancellation and pin rules apply; local-only mode cannot silently switch to a cloud reader. Missing support, timeout or poor image quality leaves the original usable and reports a concrete limitation.

A personal upload in temporary Chat is temporary working material, separate from reusable public-source cache. It may use bounded private disk staging with cleanup under the temporary session/job lifecycle; it is not silently made a durable dossier or shared with another conversation. Saving as research retains the chosen original, derived views, transcription revisions and relevant provenance under that dossier's lifecycle. Exports include only selected image/text material and applicable source-use limits. A photograph can be studied while provenance is unknown, with that status visible; reading its text does not establish that it depicts an original ancient object.

Practical checks use a few real examples: a clear page, rotated or cropped text, a blurred or damaged span, historical handwriting and mixed RTL/LTR lines. Verify that unreadable letters remain uncertain, selected regions and omissions match the image, manual corrections survive reopening, and a supplied transcription stays distinct. Check image-processing disclosure, local-only behavior and cancellation as part of the same workflow. These are source checks the user can perform, not an expert-certification program.

## Hybrid model responsibilities

The proposed product supports local execution and configured cloud coordination. A powerful API model handles conversation, synthesis, tool planning, and weighing interpretations. Local source services own retrieval, editions, citation validation, metadata, and dossiers. Local embedding/reranking and candidate translation workers support the coordinator. A compact local general model remains an offline/private alternative with separately reported capability limits.

| Role | Proposed location | Evidence/quality boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main conversation/research coordinator | Selected cloud API, or configured local alternative | Helps investigate and compare arguments; the user can inspect and question its output |
| Critical review of a difficult analysis | Optional second model family/provider | Same evidence bundle; disagreement and sources remain visible; agreement is not proof |
| Scripture and manuscript catalog, dossiers | Local metadata/storage; authorized remote scans as needed | Named editions, image provenance, rights-aware storage/export |
| Exact search, embeddings, reranking | Local | Measure retrieval by collection/language; do not substitute ranking for truth |
| Candidate translation workers | Local | Check each historical-language/direction on real examples; retain published translations for comparison |
| Image reading and transcription | Available local OCR/HTR worker or selected eligible image-capable route | Machine reading remains unreviewed until the user checks the identified scope; no invented letters, coordinates or authenticity claims |
| Exact quotations and reference checks | Deterministic local services | Quote bytes/ranges from recorded source editions; image-derived readings link to their image and transcription revision, not automatically to a verified canonical quotation; semantic support is assessed separately |

Candidate local translators already in the main plan include Helsinki-NLP OPUS-MT Bible models for Semitic and Indo-European language groups. The [Semitic model card](https://huggingface.co/Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-tc-bible-big-sem-deu_eng_nld) lists Biblical Hebrew/Aramaic among its source tags and DE/EN/NL targets; the [Indo-European model card](https://huggingface.co/Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-tc-bible-big-ine-deu_eng_nld) lists Ancient Greek and Latin. These language tags and Bible training do not establish translation accuracy, superiority over frontier models, or suitability for Quranic Arabic. Compare local candidates with named published translations and a general model on a small set of useful passages, including negation, ambiguous terms and surrounding context. Record observed errors and label generated translations; specialist review can be sought for a difficult question without becoming a prerequisite for using the tool. No reliable Quranic-Arabic specialist has been established by this planning work. Local workers may use CPU/GPU according to measured runtime; cloud reasoning does not make all translation workers cloud services.

## Provider pool and practical model comparison

The user has API keys; do not request them in chat or commit them. Configure keys through backend-only local settings/secret storage. No credentials have been inspected or configured in this work. Support a provider adapter pool rather than hardcoding one model into every persona. Record model identity, endpoint, region, API version and useful run settings so the user knows which configuration produced an answer.

The following official catalogs were checked on 8 November 2026. Availability in a catalog is not confirmation of access under the user's account or region, nor a performance endorsement:

| Candidate route | Verified catalog examples / scope |
| --- | --- |
| [Mistral API](https://docs.mistral.ai/models) | Mistral Large 4; also lists hosted Z.ai GLM 5.3. Model developer and serving provider are separate facts. |
| [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio](https://help.aliyun.com/en/model-studio/models) | qwen3.8-max and GLM-5.3 listings; check the actual regional endpoint/model identifier during setup. |
| [OpenAI API](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models) | Select and pin an available general reasoning model; compare useful example questions using the same evidence. |
| [Anthropic API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview) | Claude Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 appear in the current catalog; select by measured task quality, cost, and latency. |
| [MiniMax API](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/text-generation) | MiniMax-M3.1-Flash and MiniMax-M3; check real task behavior and tool support during setup. |
| [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) | Optional gateway, not another model family. Pin/allowlist actual upstream providers, enforce selected data policies, and disable unapproved fallback. |

Try a small, reusable set of the user's research questions covering exact citations, context, alternative readings, counterevidence, personal conversation, languages and cost. Open the cited passages and compare answers side by side, preferably with the same evidence. Keep concise notes on strengths and errors and choose a default or role assignment that is useful for the task. No fixed dataset size, locked release battery, expert panel or certification is required for personal use. More extensive benchmarking is optional. Do not claim a winner based on vendor benchmarks, model nationality or fluent theological prose. Do not invoke all providers on every ordinary chat turn.

Cloud coordination is explicitly enabled by the user and visibly identified. Selected conversation context and evidence leave the machine for that run; web-search query minimization does not mean a remote conversation model never sees personal messages. Source licensing must permit the actual external processing. Manuscript images and personal photographs are not sent for vision processing without the user's image-processing choice, applicable source-use conditions and a selected image-capable route. Show the actual crop/full-image scope and provider; a general text-model connection does not authorize every image transfer. Local-only mode blocks cloud model calls; web and remote manuscript access have separate controls and can also be disabled. Display these independent connection states accurately.

Use separate user-selected primary/reviewer routes, context/output limits, token and monetary budgets, timeout/cancel behavior, and usage accounting. Per-run costs distinguish estimates from provider-reported usage. No silent provider changes, cloud fallback, or escalating multi-model loop after failure. Resolve and record aliases such as latest where possible; observations about an older version are not automatically observations about its replacement. A changed route gets fresh practical spot checks when relevant, without blocking ordinary use pending an expert benchmark. A reviewer is a fallible critic, not a second source of historical evidence.

## User-moderated Roundtable

### Orchestrator allocation: connected models only

The general-model orchestrator can propose or automatically allocate persona/perspective roles across the user's configured model connections. It chooses only from a backend-provided eligible model registry, never an unrestricted list of model names or arbitrary URLs. Provider connection and individual model eligibility are separate: a connected provider does not mean every catalog model is accessible or enabled.

Before suitability ranking, the backend filters candidates by user-configured connection, enabled provider and model, account/region availability, required capabilities, selected data-processing settings, actual source-processing restrictions, and remaining budget. Prior expert evaluation is not an eligibility requirement for personal use. The orchestrator itself must use an eligible configured route. Credentials are not included in its prompt. Merely existing in a vendor catalog, appearing in training data, or being suggested by another participant never makes a model eligible.

Rank eligible candidates using the user's choices and recorded practical observations for the role/task/language, then cost and latency preferences. Untested matches are explicitly provisional and remain selectable; do not state that Mistral Large 4, Qwen3.8-Max, or another model is objectively best for a persona. Persona, analytical perspective, and model remain independent. Multiple roles can share a model; do not imply independent experts merely because their prompts differ. Local translation/retrieval workers retain separate capability notes and examples.

The user can inspect the proposed role/model/provider mapping, override a role, or pin it. Automatic allocation is an enabled session option under the chosen provider/data/budget policy; it is not permission to activate new providers. Record each assignment's configuration version, capability or observed-quality basis, and subsequent changes. Show a concise selection explanation rather than fabricated hidden reasoning. Backend validation checks every requested route ID again before executing a turn and rejects model output naming an ineligible route.

On disconnection, quota failure, loss of capability, or a disabled model, mark the affected role unavailable and pause it. An explicitly authorized fallback policy may choose another eligible connected model only for an unpinned role and must show the reassignment. A pinned role stays paused until the user explicitly unpins or reassigns it, even when an approved alternative is available. The backend blocks automatic reassignment and calls to a replacement model while the pin holds; explicit user reassignment still passes the same eligibility checks. No eligible candidate produces a clear unassigned state, not an invented response or an automatically enabled service. Disabling a route prevents subsequent calls; a cancellation attempt cannot retract context already sent in an in-flight request.

Allocation is separate from moderation. The orchestrator prepares assignments and performs only moderator-requested rounds/turns. It cannot advance an unlimited debate or remove human control over speaking order, pause, and synthesis.

Practical routing checks: disabled/disconnected/unavailable models are never selected; one accessible model can serve multiple roles; an untested but otherwise eligible model can be chosen with a provisional label; an empty eligible registry pauses without outbound model calls; prompt injection cannot introduce an external route; missing tool support is filtered; pin/override survives a new round; approved fallback for an unpinned role is visible and remains in the eligible registry; unapproved fallback and automatic changes to a pinned assignment are blocked; budget/local-only constraints apply to orchestrator and participants; and version changes remain visible rather than inheriting old quality observations.

Pinned-failure case: an assigned model fails while its role is pinned, an approved fallback policy is enabled, and another eligible connected model is available. The role remains paused, its assignment and pin stay intact, and no call goes to a replacement model. The UI offers explicit unpinning or reassignment; user-selected routes still pass eligibility checks and turns remain subject to moderator control. Repeat without the pin to verify that authorized fallback can select the alternative and visibly record the change.

Within Chat, offer **One-to-one** and **Roundtable** as explicit interaction choices. Research can remain open beside either. Do not automatically turn a personal grief conversation into a debate. The human user is moderator; models are labeled AI participants, not actual scholars, clergy, prophets, or representatives of an entire faith.

Configure each participant along three independent axes: **persona** (method/tone), **perspective** (a stated analytical or attributed interpretive lens), and **provider/model** (execution engine). All share the same source policy and impartiality constraints. A textual researcher, historical-context analyst, linguist, comparative scholar, and critical interlocutor are useful roles. Religious perspectives may be examined with explicit attribution, without granting them an override of the primary-text and hadith admission rules.

The moderator can select participants, set the question/scope, ask one or all to respond, approve the next turn, pause/stop, request evidence, challenge a claim, introduce a source through admission checks, or request a synthesis. Start with a bounded proposal of up to four participants and one response each per moderator-requested round, with explicit limits adjustable before the run. No endless autonomous discussion, hidden paid calls, or automatic declaration of a winner.

All participants receive the same versioned evidence dossier. New sources are logged and shared before dependent rebuttals; identify what changed. Each substantive claim links to evidence items; distinguish a participant's hypothesis from an established observation. Maintain agreement/disagreement and unresolved-question records. A requested synthesis preserves disagreements and their reasons, rather than deciding by majority vote. Different prompts on the same base model are not independent experts; different model families also may share biases.

The UI shows participant name, persona, analytical perspective, provider/model, speaking state, citations, and relevant usage. Model-call outputs are ordinary messages, not authentic historical testimony or disclosed hidden reasoning. Queue one speaker by default so the moderator can follow and control the discussion. Parallel independent opening analyses may be an explicit option within the same authorized budget; rebuttals depend on the shared preceding messages and evidence revision.

- **U4:** collection-wide related/counterevidence retrieval, source-policy enforcement before corpus/web evidence enters a bundle, distinct web/manuscript candidates and duplicate detection. - **U5:** hybrid coordinator/reviewer orchestration, all persona constraints, inspectable evidence argument, alternative-reading comparison, user-moderated Roundtable and bounded speaking queue.
- **U6:** versioned evidence items, interpretation comparisons, participant messages and explicit dossier saving/export within rights constraints; retain chosen original images, derivations, machine readings and user corrections with their R28 dossier, without silently preserving temporary Chat uploads.
- **U7:** practical checks on useful research examples and real app flows: exact sources/citations, context and counterevidence, attributed interpretations, fact-versus-hypothesis labels, translation limitations, excluded-source leakage, local/cloud settings, costs, moderator control and pin behavior. R28 examples also cover uncertain/unreadable or omitted image text, mixed scripts, region correspondence, correction history and private-image processing. Fix observed failures and keep simple reusable examples; no statistical neutrality certification or mandatory reviewer panel.
- **U8:** verify actual menu startup and provider settings without requiring a cloud key for offline reading; no model or scan download hidden inside app startup.

For a usable personal version, exercise a complete research path with real sources: question, passages, comparison, available manuscript, notes and dossier reopening. Also exercise R28 from an imported photo through a machine reading, an explicit correction and a separate translation back to the same image region. Check citations and selected examples from the collections and languages actually used, plus moderator controls and local/cloud settings. Restricted or inaccessible material keeps a source/viewer link and a clear limitation. Untried languages, sources and model combinations remain usable where technically supported with honest labels; they are not claimed to have been checked. Specialist consultation or a larger benchmark can support a later difficult research question, but is not a prerequisite for the personal tool.
